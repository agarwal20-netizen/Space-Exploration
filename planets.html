Solar System Catalog

The 8 Major Planets

Discover the unique geology, atmospheric composition, orbital distances, and surprising mysteries of our solar system's primary planets.

1. Mercury

Terrestrial

The smallest planet in the solar system and closest to the Sun. Despite being closest, it is not the hottest planet.

Distance from Sun: 57.9 Million km
Orbital Period: 88 Earth Days

Fun Fact: Mercury has no moons and experiences temperature swings from 430°C to -180°C!

2. Venus

Terrestrial

Earth's twin in size, enveloped by thick carbon dioxide clouds causing a runaway greenhouse effect.

Distance from Sun: 108.2 Million km
Orbital Period: 225 Earth Days

Fun Fact: Venus rotates in the opposite direction to most planets, and its day is longer than its year!

3. Earth

Habitable Zone

Our home world. The only place in the known universe confirmed to harbor liquid water oceans and living organisms.

Distance from Sun: 149.6 Million km
Orbital Period: 365.25 Days

Fun Fact: Earth's atmosphere shields us from meteoroids, most of which burn up as shooting stars.

4. Mars

The Red Planet

A dusty, cold desert world with a thin atmosphere. Home to Olympus Mons, the largest volcano in the solar system.

Distance from Sun: 227.9 Million km
Orbital Period: 687 Earth Days

Fun Fact: Mars gets its reddish tint from iron oxide (rust) covering its surface!

5. Jupiter

Gas Giant

More than twice as massive as all other planets combined. Famous for its iconic Great Red Spot storm larger than Earth.

Distance from Sun: 778.5 Million km
Orbital Period: 11.86 Earth Years

Fun Fact: Jupiter has 95 officially recognized moons, including Europa with its subsurface ocean!

6. Saturn

Ringed Giant

Adorned with a dazzling system of icy rings. It is the least dense planet in our solar system—so light it would float in water!

Distance from Sun: 1.43 Billion km
Orbital Period: 29.45 Earth Years

Fun Fact: Saturn's rings are made up of billions of chunks of ice, rock, and dust particles.

7. Uranus

Ice Giant

An ice giant with a unique tilt: it rotates almost completely on its side, resulting in extreme 21-year-long seasonal shifts.

Distance from Sun: 2.87 Billion km
Orbital Period: 84 Earth Years

Fun Fact: Methane in Uranus' upper atmosphere absorbs red light, giving it a cool cyan color.

8. Neptune

Deep Blue Giant

Dark, cold, and whipped by supersonic winds reaching up to 2,100 km/h. The most distant major planet in our solar system.

Distance from Sun: 4.5 Billion km
Orbital Period: 164.8 Earth Years

Fun Fact: Neptune was the first planet located through mathematical predictions before being seen!
